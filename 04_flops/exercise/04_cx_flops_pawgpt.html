PawGPT: LLMs for Pets

You woke up realizing humans hog all the LLMs — pets deserve their own! Build, budget, and scale a model that speaks fluent woof & meow.

Welcome, founder!

Every LLM today is trained on human text. But dogs bark, cats meow, and neither cares about tax law. You've decided to launch PawGPT — the first large language model for pets.

Three knobs control how smart your model feels — and how much math it needs:

SymbolWhat it means
N (parameters)Total learnable weights in the model
T (sequence length)How many tokens fit in one conversation at once
L (layers)How many times the model refines its understanding
Visualization — where N, T and L live in a transformer

T tokens go in at the bottom and travel up through L identical blocks; the weights in every block add up to N. Cost grows with all three.

In the next two sections you will derive from scratch the two formulas that govern training cost:

Linear cost (full training):  6 · N · Ttrain
Attention cost (per sequence):  12 · L · d · T²  (T = context length)

Behind the scenes, all of this boils down to one question: how much math (FLOPs) can you afford?

This exercise walks you through the math — step by step — then lets you unlock GPUs, scale up, and finally pitch investors.

Part 1a — Deriving 6 · N · T

Most of PawGPT's compute is plain matrix multiplication. Let's build up to the full training formula one question at a time.
Notation: N = total parameters  |  d = layer width  |  T = total training tokens (all woofs + meows in the dataset)

Every linear layer multiplies an input vector by a weight matrix. One multiply-add = 2 FLOPs (one multiply + one add):

FLOPs (one linear layer, one token) = 2 × input_size × output_size
Visualization — from one linear layer to 2N

A d×d weight matrix has N = d² parameters. Each is used once in a multiply-add → 2N FLOPs for the forward pass.

Part 1b — Deriving 12 · L · d · T²

Attention is where PawGPT compares every token to every other token — that's why long conversations get expensive, fast.
Notation: L = number of transformer layers  |  d = hidden dimension  |  T = sequence length (context window, in tokens)

Self-attention builds a T × T matrix: each of T tokens scores against all T tokens. Two heavy matrix multiplies create the T² term:

① Q KT  →  (T×d)(d×T)  →  2 · T² · d FLOPs
② Attn · V  →  (T×T)(T×d)  →  2 · T² · d FLOPs
The T × T attention grid
Grid size16 × 16
Total entries256T²

Each cell = one token asking "how relevant is this other token?" — T tokens → T² comparisons.

Matrix shapes in attention

Both steps produce a T×T intermediate — that's the T² bottleneck.

Why T² hurts — how FLOPs scale with L, d, and T

L and d scale costs gently. Context length T scales as T² — doubling the window multiplies attention FLOPs by four.

Part 2 — Unlock your training GPUs

Your demo PawGPT: 1 billion parameters, 1K token context window. You plan to train on 20 billion woof/meow tokens (the "Chinchilla" sweet spot ≈ 20× params).
QuantityValueFormula
Training FLOPs—6 × N × T (T = training tokens)
Inference FLOPs (full context)—2 × N × T (T = context length)

To rent a GPU, the cloud provider asks: "How many hours will training take, and how fast is inference?" Calculate correctly to unlock each card.

Training time
hours = 6 × N × Ttrain Peak FLOPs/s  ×  MFUtrain  ×  3600
The 3600 converts seconds into hours.
Inference time
seconds = Inference FLOPs Peak FLOPs/s  ×  MFUinf
  • Peak FLOPs/s — the spec-sheet top speed of the GPU: the most floating-point operations it can theoretically perform per second.
  • MFU (Model FLOPs Utilisation) — the fraction of that peak speed you actually reach in practice, once memory traffic, communication and idle time are accounted for.
